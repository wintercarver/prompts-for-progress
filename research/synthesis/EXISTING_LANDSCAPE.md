# Existing landscape and differentiation

## Closest existing efforts

### Erdős problems AI-contributions wiki

The [community wiki](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/wiki/AI-contributions-to-Erd%C5%91s-problems) is the strongest known living case index in a single mathematical collection. It already separates full, partial, incorrect, rediscovered, and formalized contributions and publishes extensive interpretive caveats.

Prompts for Progress should complement rather than copy it: preserve run-level prompt and workflow provenance, normalize cross-field metadata, and retain time-stamped snapshots of how validation evolves.

### Survey of verifiable mathematical discovery

The [BigPicture 2026 survey](https://aclanthology.org/2026.bigpicture-main.2/) offers a curated mathematical timeline, an evidence ladder, recurring system patterns, and a reproducibility checklist. It is a high-value seed source and conceptual reference, but it is a static paper rather than a living prompt archive.

### FrontierMath: Open Problems

[FrontierMath: Open Problems](https://epoch.ai/frontiermath/open-problems/about) preregisters open problems, their expected significance, and programmatic verifiers. It also publishes unsuccessful model attempts. Its scope is benchmarking automatically verifiable mathematics rather than documenting the broader history of AI-assisted research.

### General AI-assisted paper archives

[ai.viXra.org](https://ai.vixra.org/) accepts self-described AI-assisted scholarly papers at high volume. It demonstrates both the scale of possible material and the need for strong evidence, attribution, and editorial distinctions. Paper presence alone is not adequate evidence of a research contribution.

### Open-problem maps and registries

[openproblems.ai](https://openproblems.ai/) aims to map open mathematics and physics problems, while formal registries such as theorem-prover task collections organize machine-verifiable targets. These focus on problems to be solved, not the provenance of attempts and results.

## Proposed differentiation

Prompts for Progress should make the **attempt** the central empirical object while linking attempts to shared research cases or problems. Its distinctive combination is:

- successful, partial, disputed, rediscovered, and unsuccessful attempts;
- prompts and workflows as preserved artifacts;
- role-specific human and organizational attribution;
- changing validation events rather than a timeless verdict;
- cross-domain coverage;
- machine-readable records designed for later aggregate analysis;
- a public timeline that shows documented activity while exposing coverage bias.
