## Seed collections located

- [AI contributions to Erdős problems](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/wiki/AI-contributions-to-Erd%C5%91s-problems), a large and frequently updated community index that includes full results, partial results, rediscoveries, incorrect attempts, formalizations, and computational contributions.
- [Open Problems Solved by LLMs? A Survey of Verifiable Mathematical Discovery](https://aclanthology.org/2026.bigpicture-main.2/), a July 2026 peer-reviewed survey with a case timeline, evidence ladder, and reproducibility checklist.
- [Semi-Autonomous Mathematics Discovery with Gemini](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22401), a 700-problem Erdős scan reporting both seemingly novel solutions and prior-art rediscoveries.
- [FrontierMath: Open Problems](https://epoch.ai/frontiermath/open-problems/about), a preregistered open-problem benchmark that publishes model attempts and explicit interpretation caveats.
- [Our First Proof submissions](https://openai.com/index/first-proof-submissions/), ten research-level proof attempts with expert feedback and a paper containing prompt patterns.
- [Advancing Mathematics Research with AI-Driven Formal Proof Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22763), reporting open Erdős and OEIS results backed by formal verification.
- [ai.viXra.org](https://ai.vixra.org/), a high-volume archive of self-described AI-assisted papers useful for discovery but not as validation evidence.
- [Open Problems](https://openproblems.ai/), an announced mathematics and physics problem map whose full datasets were not yet publicly available when checked.

## Early observations

1. The Erdős wiki is already a rich source of unsuccessful and disputed attempts, not merely successes. It should be treated as a discovery index and living expert assessment, then traced to primary artifacts case by case.
2. A single problem can have multiple materially different runs, contributors, outcomes, and later validations. The archive will need separate concepts for a **research case/problem** and an **attempt/run**.
3. Rediscovery and prior-art detection are common enough to require explicit representation rather than being folded into success or failure.
4. Formal verification establishes consistency with a formal statement but does not by itself establish that the formal statement captures the intended research question, that the result is novel, or that it is important.
5. FrontierMath's attempt pages provide unusually useful documented non-successes and a model for preregistering problem importance and verification criteria.
6. Counts on the eventual site must be framed as archive coverage, not the underlying incidence or success rate of AI-assisted research.

## Access and coverage limitations

- Initial search was English-language and web-index dependent.
- Social platforms and shared chats may be incompletely indexed or mutable.
- Company reports may omit full transcripts, internal prompts, compute details, and negative trials.
- New cases are appearing rapidly; every release requires a cutoff date and later update pass.
